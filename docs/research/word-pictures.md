# Word pictures

**Question.** In the Colors and Weather units, should the cue under a sentence be a picture of the word's meaning instead of its English word, and which words can a drawing show without a second meaning?

**Date.** 2026-11-23

## What it decides

- In the Colors and Weather word units, the cue under the sentence is a picture of the meaning instead of the English word.
- Only words a drawing shows unambiguously get a picture. Every other word keeps the English.
- Colors show the color itself as paint, one swatch for each of the 13 paints.
- Weather shows one of five drawn scenes: sun, rain, snow, wind and cloud.

## Evidence and limits

- Paivio (1971), _Imagery and Verbal Processes_, and Paivio (1986), _Mental Representations_: dual-coding theory. Words linked to images are remembered better than words alone.
- Lotto & de Groot (1998), “Effects of learning method and word type on acquiring vocabulary in an unfamiliar language,” _Language Learning_ 48(1): word–word and picture–word learning were compared. Picture learning was not better overall in that study.
- Carpenter & Olson (2012), “Are pictures good for learning new vocabulary in a foreign language? Only if you think they are not,” _Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning, Memory, and Cognition_ 38(1): pictures help only under some conditions, and learners overestimate how much pictures help.

The evidence that pictures beat translations is mixed. Lanna uses a picture where it takes the English out of the cue, so the learner retrieves the word from its meaning rather than from the first language (P3). It keeps the English wherever a picture could mean more than one thing.

## Blind review

Colors needed no review beyond checking that the 13 paints tell apart in Day and Night.

The five weather scenes were reviewed blind by a Claude reviewer and a Gemini 3.8 Flash reviewer, final round of 7. Scores are reviewers who named the intended word.

- Shipped: rain 5/5, snow 5/5, cloud 4/5, sun 4/5, wind 4/4.
- Dropped, so they keep the English: warm, weather, cold, hot and umbrella. Each could be read as another word.
- Not drawn: sky.

## Not verified

- No learning outcome has been measured in Lanna. Whether word pictures help these learners is open.
- The review checks whether a drawing reads as one word. It does not check whether the picture teaches the word.
