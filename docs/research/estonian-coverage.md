# Estonian course coverage, A1 to B2

Research for the hand rewrite of the Estonian course. Pages fetched 2026-11-08.

**Learner:** English-speaking adult. Knows Slovak (about B1) and Russian (A1). Estonian is about A2. Goal: understand spoken Estonian first, then hold a conversation at B1.

**How to read the tables**

- **Level · basis** gives the CEFR level where the item is introduced, then the source for that placement. A2R and BR are Harno's official reference level descriptions (section 1). KK*n* is Keeleklikk chapter _n_. KT*n* is Keeletee chapter _n_.
- No official source describes Estonian A1 grammar. The A2 description is the first one. Keeleklikk covers A1 and A2 but does not mark which chapter is which. "A1" in these tables therefore means Keeleklikk chapters 1–8. That split is **proposed**, not stated by Keeleklikk.
- **Typical mistake:** "doc." means a source records the error. "pred." means a prediction from comparing English (and Slovak) with Estonian; no source checks it.
- **Src** codes point to section 1. EKKR chapter codes such as M56 or SÜ36 open as `https://arhiiv.eki.ee/books/ekkr/m56.html` or `.../sy36.html`.
- "not verified" marks every claim I could not check against a fetched page.
- Examples are quoted from the cited source unless the row says "constructed by rule".

### 3.11 Verbs with particles (_ühendverbid_)

Sources: EKKR M10 https://arhiiv.eki.ee/books/ekkr/m10.html · SV entries, e.g. https://sonaveeb.ee/search/unif/dlall/dsall/l%C3%A4bi%20lugema/1/est

Rule: a verb + particle form one unit with a new or narrower meaning. The particle usually goes to the end of a main clause and stands before an infinitive (SV examples). Perfective particles: _ära, läbi, valmis, ilma_. Many particles form to – at – from triads (_kokku – koos – koost_, _kõrvale – kõrval – kõrvalt_) (M10). Typical mistake (pred.): putting the particle right after the verb, as in English "read through the letter".

### 5.5 What the official A2 list requires that no unit covers

From A2R pp. 69–77 and Keeleklikk, with the inventory item to use:

- Cases: nominative plural (NOU-1), ablative (NOU-9), terminative (NOU-11), essive (NOU-12), abessive (NOU-13), short illative (NOU-16), all plural cases (NOU-19), principal parts (NOU-17), gradation as grammar (NOU-22, NOU-23; tutor only).
- Adjectives: agreement and the four-case rule (ADJ-1, ADJ-2), comparative and superlative (ADJ-4 to ADJ-6).
- Pronouns in cases other than the nominative (PRO-1; the word unit lists only nominatives), pronoun objects (PRO-2), _see_ (PRO-3).
- Numbers above six, ordinals, clock, dates, weekdays, months (NUM-2, NUM-6 to NUM-11; tutor only).
- Verbs: all present persons (VRB-11), negation (VRB-13, VRB-17), the three past types (VRB-14 to VRB-16), _ma/da_ infinitives (VRB-3 to VRB-5), _-mas, -mast, -mata_ (VRB-7, VRB-8, VRB-10), present conditional (VRB-21), imperative plural and negative (VRB-24 to VRB-26), impersonal present and past (VRB-30 to VRB-32), _-nud/-tud_ participles (VRB-20, VRB-36), _des_-form (VRB-37), necessity and modals (VRB-38, VRB-39).
- Object case (OBJ-1 to OBJ-8; tutor only); existential sentences and the partitive subject (SEN-2 to SEN-4); _et, sest_ clauses (SEN-18).
- Postpositions and prepositions (ADP-1 to ADP-17); particle verbs (PTV); word formation basics (WF-1, WF-2, WF-6).
