# E95. A cheatsheet's own lines can be practiced by ear, and that practice is kept apart from everything measured (2026-11-09, branch `feat/cheatsheet-listen-practice`)

**Why:** the owner is at A1 in Russian and wants to play a co-op game with a friend in Russian only: "I need to be able to understand and say whatever comes up." The phrases for it are already a cheatsheet (E50), but a sheet could only be read, played one phrase at a time, or covered. The gap that matters in a game is the ear at speed (learner.md: "I can't put them together into meaning fast enough"). The owner asked whether a 3D scene tool would help and then said to build what makes the most sense. Research note: [research/situation-practice.md](../research/situation-practice.md).

**How to apply:**
- **One screen (E8).** An open sheet has **Listen and type**, and each section has its own **Listen**. Both open the Listen screen on the sheet (`/listen?sheet=<id>`, `&section=<id>`): ten phrases, each heard with the text hidden and typed as heard, then shown with its meaning and note. Escape and the end screen go back to the sheet.
- **What a line is** (`sheetLines`, `src/core/sheet-listen.ts`): each alternative of a row is its own line ("Он настоящий? / Она настоящая?" is two), with the row's whole meaning and note. A phrase with a gap to fill (… or ...) is skipped. A phrase that appears twice is one line, in its first section. The Say it column is not shown in the exercise (the russian-language skill: no exercise in Latin letters).
- **Which ten** (`pickSheetLines`): up to five phrases missed last time, the oldest miss first; then phrases not heard yet, in the sheet's order, so a first session is the sheet's first ten; then the ones caught the fewest times in a row. The numbers are the app's own (E63).
- **Graded on the server** against the sheet's line as it stands (`recordSheetListen`), by Listen's rule (`judgeTyped`) with the running language's tag. A phrase is **caught by ear** while its last answer matched without the text shown; an answer with only a mark off counts as caught, as in Listen (E38).
- **Apart from everything measured.** This squares E50 and E27 ("a mistake in a generated line never becomes a drill") with practicing the lines: the lines are the learner's own text and nothing checks them, so the practice feeds nothing that claims to measure the language. It earns no XP and no stamp (E31), adds nothing to Review, words known, `recent`, the Listen count of the day or the lesson log. So the listening speed (Milestones), the listening trends (E38) and the Russian study (E82) never read it. It counts as listening minutes (P9), as Today's line does (E54). The end screen says all of this in one line (P8).
- **Kept (E1):** `cheatsheets/practice/<sheet id>.json` (per phrase: times heard, caught in a row, when) and `cheatsheets/practice/log.jsonl` (every session with what was typed). The corpus export writes `cheatsheet-practice.json`.
- **E82:** Listen is one of the study's input tasks and nothing here uses the microphone, so the listening-first arm keeps it. The minutes do show in the study's daily minutes.
- **Not built, with reasons:** saying the phrases with a score and a role-play of the situation (E50's open "custom Speak scene from a sheet") wait until the study ends on 2026-12-20, because both are spoken output in the language under test. Importing a PDF: the chat can already read one, and a sheet is one line per phrase. Dialogues with their questions (the owner's workbook has four) have no home in a sheet yet. A 3D or 2D scene to click through: see the research note.
