# E90. Estonian pairs, sound notes that stay in their language, and what two blind readers changed (2026-11-10, branch `feat/ear-gym-estonian`)

**Why:** the owner: "why does Estonian have no pairs?", then "You can also fix the other issues you found as well", with the standing rule not to be consulted until the PR is ready. Estonian is studied to understand speech, like Russian. The other issues are the open points of E86 to E88 and the things the PR descriptions listed as not looked at.

**How to apply:**
- **Estonian has 41 pairs in six groups:** short and long vowels (*sada*, *saada*), short and long consonants (*lina*, *linna*; *kabi*, *kapi*), and the vowels õ, ü, ä and ö against o, u, a and o (*korv*, *kõrv*; *tuli*, *tüli*; *karu*, *käru*; *kook*, *köök*). `curriculum/estonian/phonology-pairs.json`.
- **Every Estonian word, form, meaning and example sentence was checked in a dictionary by a second agent** (Sõnaveeb with EKI's morphology, and ÕS 2018), in two passes. It confirmed the inflected forms, corrected three entries, and found a second difference in two pairs (*kull* and *küll*, *kulm* and *külm*: the l is palatalized in one word only), which were dropped.
- **A written form with two lengths** (*kooli*, *linna*, *saada* are the second or the third length, by meaning) may be the long word of a short and long pair, because both readings are long. It may not be the plain word of a vowel pair: *looma* as a genitive is the second length and *lööma* the third, so the pair uses the partitive *looma* (third length). Pairs that differ only in the second and third length, or only in a palatalized consonant, are spelled the same and stay out, like stress pairs (E87).
- **Sound notes belong to one language.** `getArticulatoryCue(symbol, languageId)` and `pronReport(scores, { languageId })`: the Slovak notes no longer show under a Russian or Estonian scene, and the link to the ear gym on the Speak end screen is there only when the language has pairs.
- **Read blind, then changed.** A fresh Claude and Gemini 3.8 Flash each saw three screenshots with no explanation. Both read the underline (the letters that differ), the ten segments, the hollow segment (a miss) and the rows on the end screen (they start a round) as meant. Their misreadings changed four things: the segment for the pair on screen is grey, not pale teal; the line under the question says "Listen for u and ü" in place of the group's name and the letters; from 640 px up the tiles are tagged "Played" and "Your pick" after an answer; and the screen says "sounds", not "contrasts" ("All sounds", "Sounds in this round").
- **320 px.** At that width the card shrank when an answer came in: the question wrapped to two lines and the hint was longer than the cue. The question is now "Which word?", the hint one sentence shorter, and the cue line keeps room for four lines below 380 px.
- **The sound menu is a quiet text control** at the end of the card head (`quiet` and `align` on `ChoiceMenu`), not a boxed field. On a phone, where the Pairs / Rhythm switch shares the top band, the count is left out and the ten segments are the count.
- **A stopped clip lets go of its source before its address is revoked** (`components/speech.tsx`): Chromium logged a failed request for a clip stopped while it was still loading, which happened whenever an answer came before the word had finished loading.
- **Two tests that failed on main pass.** `tests/provider-tracing.test.ts` names the two folders `next.config.ts` has shipped with the app since the Estonian and Russian courses. `tests/credits.test.ts` took a forum address in a comment of the Clozemaster export for a source the app fetches from; it is listed in `HOSTS_THAT_ARE_NOT_SOURCES`.
- **Still open:** Russian stress pairs (the voice cannot be told where the stress is); no Slovak pair for `l` and `ľa`; nobody has listened to any pair; no native speaker has read the words; the rhythm checker's exceptions are a short list. Seen while testing and not part of this work: the app's notices ("Language added") cover the bar at the bottom of a task screen on a phone.
