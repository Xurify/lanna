# Russian Syntax and Style

Russian sentence structure features grammatically flexible word order steered by Functional Sentence Perspective (Topic-Comment structure), impersonal predicate architectures, and nuanced subordinate conjunctions.

## 1. Word Order and Information Structure (FSP)

Because grammatical roles are marked by case endings, Russian word order is functionally pragmatic rather than fixed:
- **Theme / Topic (*Данное*):** Known information placed at the start of the clause.
- **Rheme / Comment (*Новое*):** New, informative, or emphasized information placed at the end of the clause. Intonational center falls on the sentence-final element.

### Definite vs. Indefinite Nuance
Without definite or indefinite articles, Russian uses word order to express definiteness:
- *Студент вошёл в комнату.* (The student entered the room. Topic = the student).
- *В комнату вошёл студент.* (A student entered the room. Comment = a student; introducing a new character).

### Existential vs. Locative Sentences
- **Location of known subject:** `Subject + Verb + Place`:
  - *Книга лежит на столе.* (The book is on the table.)
- **Presence of entity in space (Existential):** `Place + Verb + Subject`:
  - *На столе лежит книга.* (There is a book on the table.)

## 2. Impersonal and Dative Predicates

Russian frequently expresses personal experiences, sensations, moral obligations, and permissions without a nominative subject. The experiencing person is in the **Dative case**:

```
Dative Subject + Predicate (Adverb / Modal) + (Infinitive / Clause)
```

| Type | Structure | Russian Example | English Equivalent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Physical sensation** | `Dat + Adverb` | *Мне холодно / жарко / душно.* | I am cold / hot / stifled. |
| **Psychological state** | `Dat + Adverb` | *Ей скучно / весело / грустно.* | She is bored / cheerful / sad. |
| **Necessity / Obligation** | `Dat + надо / нужно + Inf` | *Нам надо уходить.* | We must leave. |
| **Permission** | `Dat + можно + Inf` | *Вам можно войти.* | You may come in. |
| **Prohibition** | `Dat + нельзя + Inf` | *Детям нельзя смотреть этот фильм.* | Children must not watch this movie. |
| **Age** | `Dat + Number + года/лет` | *Моему брату двадцать пять лет.* | My brother is 25 years old. |

*Tense in Impersonal Predicates:*
- Present: zero copula (*Мне холодно. Ему нужно идти.*)
- Past: neuter *было* (*Мне было холодно. Ему нужно было идти.*)
- Future: neuter *будет* (*Мне будет холодно. Ему нужно будет идти.*)

## 3. Negation Syntax

### 1. General Sentence Negation with *Не*
Place *не* immediately before the word being negated:
- *Я **не** знаю этого человека.* (I don't know this person.)
- *Он приехал **не** вчера, а позавчера.* (He arrived not yesterday, but the day before.)

### 2. Negative Pronouns and Double Negation
Russian requires negative pronouns (*никто, ничто, никогда, нигде, никуда, никакой*) to co-occur with *не* before the verb:
- *Я **ничего не** понимаю.* (I don't understand anything.)
- *Он **никогда никому ничего не** рассказывал.* (He never told anyone anything.)

When combined with prepositions, the negative prefix splits:
- *ни с кем* (with no one), *ни о чём* (about nothing), *ни у кого* (at no one's).

### 3. Negation of Existence (*Нет / Не было / Не будет*)
Governs the **Genitive case**:
- Present: *У меня **нет** словаря.* (I don't have a dictionary.)
- Past: *Вчера в классе **не было** учителя.* (Yesterday the teacher wasn't in class.)
- Future: *Завтра дождя **не будет**.* (Tomorrow there will be no rain.)

## 4. Complex Sentences and Subordination

### 1. Purpose with *Чтобы*
- If the subject of both clauses is the same: `чтобы + Infinitive`:
  - *Я пришёл, **чтобы помочь** вам.* (I came to help you.)
- If the subjects differ: `чтобы + Past Tense`:
  - *Я хочу, **чтобы ты прочитал** эту статью.* (I want you to read this article.)

### 2. Conditionals with *Если* and *Если бы*
- Real condition: *Если погода будет хорошей, мы пойдём гулять.*
- Counterfactual / Unreal condition: *Если бы у меня было время, я поехал бы в Казань.*

### 3. Indirect Questions with *Ли*
Particle *ли* functions as "whether / if", placed immediately after the queried predicate:
- *Я не знаю, **придёт ли** он завтра.* (I don't know whether he will come tomorrow.)
- *Спроси у него, **был ли** он в Москве.* (Ask him whether he has been to Moscow.)
