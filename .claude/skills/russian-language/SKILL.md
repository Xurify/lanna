# Russian Language (Русский язык)

Produce idiomatic standard Russian, explain choices at the learner's level, and preserve the speaker's intended register. Use TORFL / ТРКИ (CEFR-aligned) levels as flexible guidance, not as a substitute for context-sensitive editing.

## Select the task mode

| Request           | Do                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Translate         | Establish audience, `ты`/`вы`, and register. Translate meaning and natural collocation, not words one by one. Distinguish motion verbs and aspect accurately in context.                                                                            |
| Write or rewrite  | State the register, use natural Russian discourse particles (`же, ведь, вот, ну`), and proofread case government, aspect pairs, animacy, and preposition variants (`в/во, с/со, к/ко, о/об/обо`).                                                   |
| Correct           | Return a corrected version first. Then give high-value explanations, labelled by error type (case government, aspect, motion verbs, agreement, vowel reduction/spelling). Preserve natural colloquial phrasing unless formal standard is requested. |
| Teach             | Diagnose the learner's level with a short production task. Teach one controlled pattern (e.g. spatial triad `где / куда / откуда` or unidirectional vs multidirectional motion), contrast with near misses, then elicit new sentences.              |
| Plan study        | Map the learner's goal to an A1–B2/C1 progression aligned with official ТРКИ (TORFL) standards. Combine retrieval, spaced review, listening, speaking, reading, and writing.                                                                        |
| Explain grammar   | Give the functional meaning first, then the minimal paradigm (nominative, genitive, and accusative/prepositional anchors, or infinitive + 1sg/2sg verb stems) and 3–5 contextual examples.                                                          |
| Author curriculum | Validate sentences against authentic usage, ensure valid cloze tokens, correct case government, and check all standard variant answers.                                                                                                             |

Always write Russian in Cyrillic. In pedagogical contexts or teaching explanations, mark stress with the combining acute accent (U+0301) on words of two or more syllables (e.g. `за́мок / замо́к`), and always write `ё` (`всё, её, ещё`).

## Mandatory production pass

Before finalizing meaningful Russian output, check:

1. **Animacy & case agreement:** In the accusative, masculine animate nouns take the genitive ending in singular and plural (`видел студента / студентов`), while inanimate take the nominative (`видел стол / столы`). All animate plurals (masculine, feminine, neuter) share accusative = genitive (`видел сестёр, кошек`).
2. **Aspect choice (вид глагола):** Perfective (_совершенный вид_) denotes a completed single event, boundary, or concrete sequence; imperfective (_несовершенный вид_) denotes process, duration, habit, state, or simple naming of the fact (_констатация факта_). In negative imperatives, use imperfective for general prohibition (`не делай!`) and perfective only for warning against unintentional failure (`не упади!`).
3. **Verbs of motion (глаголы движения):** Never conflate unidirectional (_идти, ехать, бежать_) with multidirectional/habitual (_ходить, ездить, бегать_). Distinguish foot travel (_идти/ходить_) from vehicle transport (_ехать/ездить_). When prefixes attach, unidirectional verbs become perfective with directional focus (`поехать, приехать, уехать`), while multidirectional stems form regular imperfective aspectual partners (`приезжать, уезжать`).
4. **Numeral-noun agreement (паукальные числительные):** Numbers ending in 1 (except 11) govern NOMINATIVE SINGULAR (`один стол, двадцать один стол, одна книга`). Numbers ending in 2, 3, 4 (except 12–14) govern GENITIVE SINGULAR (`два стола, двадцать два стола, три книги, четыре слова`). Numbers 5–20 and compound numbers ending in 5–9 or 0 govern GENITIVE PLURAL (`пять столов, двадцать пять книг`). In oblique cases, numeral and noun agree in case and plural number (`с двумя друзьями`).
5. **Government & mobile prepositions:** Ensure verbs govern their required case (`бояться + genitive`, `помогать + dative`, `управлять + instrumental`, `думать о + prepositional`). Use expanded prepositions before specific phonetic clusters (`со мной, во вторник, ко мне, обо мне, об этом`).
6. **Spelling rules:** Observe the 7-letter rule (after `г, к, х, ж, ч, ш, щ` write `и`, never `ы`) and the 5-letter rule (after `ж, ч, ш, щ, ц` write unstressed `е`, never `о`, except in unassimilated foreign loanwords like _шокола́д, шофёр, шоссе́_).
7. **Spatial triads (`где? куда? откуда?`):**
   - Location (_где?_): `в / на + prepositional` (`в городе, на вокзале`).
   - Destination (_куда?_): `в / на + accusative` (`в город, на вокзал`).
   - Origin (_откуда?_): `из` pairs with `в`, `с/со` pairs with `на` + genitive (`из города, с вокзала`).
8. **Interference:** Avoid English calques (Russian rarely uses dummy subjects; "it is cold" is impersonal `холодно`). Avoid false friends with other Slavic languages (Russian `чёрствый` = stale, not fresh; `овощи` = vegetables, not fruit; `позор` = disgrace, not attention; `неделя` = week, not Sunday).

## Verify instead of guessing

When any form, stress placement, aspect pair, or government is uncertain:

1. **Gramota.ru** at [gramota.ru](https://gramota.ru/): The official normative portal of the Russian language, integrating the academic dictionaries of the Russian Academy of Sciences (Zaliznyak's grammatical dictionary, Ozhegov/Shvedova, Lopatin's orthographic dictionary).
2. **Русская грамматика (Грамматика-80)** at [rusgram.narod.ru](http://rusgram.narod.ru/): The exhaustive descriptive standard by the USSR Academy of Sciences (ed. N. Yu. Shvedova).
3. **Russian National Corpus (НКРЯ)** at [ruscorpora.ru](https://ruscorpora.ru/): Authentic usage, collocation frequencies, and syntactic distribution across modern texts.
4. **ТРКИ / TORFL State Educational Standards**: Official requirements and lexical minimums for Russian as a Foreign Language (A1–B2).

If you cannot verify, indicate uncertainty explicitly and offer the safest standard alternative. Never invent an aspectual pair or irregular plural.
