# Spoken Estonian, Discourse Particles, and Register (Suuline kõne ja partiklid)

Token-minimized reference for dialogue markers, spoken contractions, and conversational register.

---

## 1. High-Frequency Discourse Particles

Estonian dialogue relies heavily on pragmatic particles to express speaker attitude, evidentiality, and expectation:

| Particle | Meaning / Function | Contextual Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ju** | "You know", "after all", appeals to shared knowledge | _Sa tead seda ju küll._ (You know that after all.)<br>_Ma ütlesin ju sulle._ (I told you so, didn't I?) |
| **küll** | 1. Reassurance ("surely, definitely")<br>2. Concession ("indeed... but") | 1. _Küll me hakkama saame._ (We'll manage just fine.)<br>2. _Ta tahtis küll tulla, aga ei jõudnud._ (He did want to come, but couldn't make it.) |
| **ometi** | "Finally", "at last", or insistence/impatience | _Tule ometi siia!_ (Do come here at last!)<br>_Ometi kord on reede._ (Finally it's Friday.) |
| **vist** | "Probably", "I suppose" | _Ta on vist kodus._ (He is probably at home.) |
| **ehk** | "Perhaps", "maybe" (polite softeners) | _Ehk saame kokku leppida?_ (Perhaps we can agree?) |
| **vaata / kuule** | Attention-getters ("Look", "Listen") | _Kuule, kas sul on hetk aega?_ (Listen, do you have a second?) |
| **noh** | Conversational pause, turn-taking filler | _Noh, hakkame siis minema._ (Well, let's get going then.) |
| **nagu** | "Like", approximate hedging / word-search marker | _See oli nagu natuke imelik._ (It was, like, a bit strange.) |

## 2. Conversational Tags & Affirmations

### The Question Tag (_või / vä / vää_)

In colloquial speech, yes/no questions are often formed by dropping _kas_ and appending _või_ or the casual clitic _vä_:

- _Tuled kaasa või?_ (Coming along, or what?)
- _Nalja teed vä?_ (Are you kidding?)
- _Said aru vä?_ (Did you understand?)

### Affirmative & Negative Responses

- **jah**: Standard factual "yes".
- **jaa**: Warmer, attentive, emphatic agreement ("yes, indeed; gladly").
- **mhmh**: Extremely common low-register vocalic assent in casual dialogue.
- **ei**: "no". Fast double negation _eiei_ softens refusals ("no no, it's fine").
- **muidugi / kindlasti**: "of course / certainly".

## 3. Spoken Reductions & Contractions (Kõnepruugi lühendid)

In authentic spontaneous speech, several morphological clusters contract regularly:

| Standard Written Form | Spoken / Casual Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| _ei ole_ | _pole_ (standard spoken) / _ple_ (rapid text) | is not / are not |
| _ei tea_ | _ma'i tea_ / _eitea_ | I don't know |
| _olnud_ | _olnd_ | been (past participle) |
| _läinud_ | _läind_ | gone |
| _tulnud_ | _tulnd_ | come |
| _võinud_ | _võind_ | could have |
| _hakkama_ | _akkama_ (initial _h-_ dropped in casual speech) | to begin |
| _hommikul_ | _ommikul_ | in the morning |

## 4. Register & Forms of Address (Sina vs. Teie)

### Sinatamine (_sina / sa_)

- Universal among family, friends, children, students, and peers.
- Standard culture in modern Estonian startups, creative industries, and casual commerce.
- Uses 2sg verb forms: _Mis sa teed?_, _Kuidas sul läheb?_

### Teietamine (_teie / te_)

- Respectful form when addressing unfamiliar adults, elderly persons, medical professionals, or official authorities.
- Uses 2pl verb forms: _Kuidas teil läheb?_, _Kas te saaksite aidata?_
- Capitalized _Teie_ is reserved for formal personal correspondence (letters and formal emails).

### Everyday Politeness Formulas

- **Aitäh! / Tänan!**: "Thank you!"
- **Suur aitäh! / Tänan väga!**: "Thank you very much!"
- **Palun!**: "Please!" / "Here you are!" / "You're welcome!"
- **Pole tänu väärt! / Võta heaks!**: "Don't mention it!" / "You're welcome!"
- **Vabanda! / Vabandage!**: "Excuse me! / I'm sorry!"
- **Head aega! / Nägemist!**: "Goodbye!"
- **Nägemiseni!**: "See you soon!"
- **Tsau! / Tšau!**: "Hi! / Bye!" (ubiquitous informal greeting).
