# Syntax, Word Order, and Clause Structure (Süntaks ja lauseõpetus)

Token-minimized reference for clause architecture based on Erelt & Metslang (_Eesti keele süntaks_, 2017).

---

## 1. Constituent Order & the V2 Principle

In neutral declarative main clauses, standard Estonian adheres to the **Verb-Second (V2)** constraint: the finite verb occupies the second structural position in the clause.

### Constituent Permutations

- **SVO (Neutral topic: Subject)**:
  - _Jüri_ [1] _ostis_ [V] _eile poest uue raamatu._ (Jüri bought a new book from the shop yesterday.)
- **Adv-V-S-O (Inverted topic: Adverbial)**:
  - _Eile_ [1] _ostis_ [V] _Jüri poest uue raamatu._ (Yesterday, Jüri bought a new book from the shop.)
  - _Poest_ [1] _ostis_ [V] _Jüri eile uue raamatu._ (From the shop, Jüri bought a new book yesterday.)
- **O-V-S-Adv (Topicalized Object)**:
  - _Uue raamatu_ [1] _ostis_ [V] _Jüri eile poest._ (The new book, Jüri bought yesterday from the shop.)

_Note: Placing an adverbial at the front automatically moves the subject after the finite verb. Never produce `*Eile Jüri ostis...` in formal standard Estonian._

## 2. Existential and Possession Clauses

Estonian sharply distinguishes between **normal predicative clauses** and **existential clauses**:

### Normal Predicative vs. Existential Clauses

| Clause Type            | Constituent Order                             | Focus / Meaning                           | Example                                            |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Normal Predicative** | Subject (known) + Verb + Location             | Location of a definite entity             | _Raamat on laual._ (The book is on the table.)     |
| **Existential**        | Location + Verb (_on_) + Subject (indefinite) | Existence or introduction of a new entity | _Laual on raamat._ (There is a book on the table.) |

### The Critical Case Shift in Negative Existential & Possession Clauses

In positive existential and possessive sentences, an indefinite subject can be in the Nominative or Partitive. In the **negative**, the subject **always shifts to the Partitive case**:

- **Positive Possession**: _Mul on auto._ (I have a car — Nominative _auto_).
- **Negative Possession**: _Mul ei ole autot._ (I don't have a car — Partitive _autot_).
- **Positive Existential**: _Kapis on leib._ (There is bread in the cupboard — Nominative _leib_).
- **Negative Existential**: _Kapis ei ole leiba._ (There is no bread in the cupboard — Partitive _leiba_).

## 3. Question Formation

Estonian employs three main interrogation strategies:

1. **General (Yes/No) Questions**: Formed by placing the question particle **kas** at the beginning of the declarative clause without changing word order:
   - _Kas sa tuled täna õhtul kinno?_ (Are you coming to the cinema tonight?)
   - _Kas te räägite inglise keelt?_ (Do you speak English?)
2. **Inverted Questions**: Common in rapid or colloquial speech:
   - _Tuled sa täna?_ (Are you coming today?)
3. **Wh-Questions (K-küsimused)**: Fronted interrogative pronoun or adverb + V2:
   - _Kus sa elad?_ (Where do you live?)
   - _Mida sa sööd?_ (What are you eating?)
   - _Millal rong saabub?_ (When does the train arrive?)
   - _Miks sa hilinesid?_ (Why were you late?)

## 4. Negation Mechanics

Sentence negation is expressed with the invariant negative particle **ei** accompanied by the **connegative stem**:

- **Present Indicative**: _ei_ + connegative verb stem:
  - _Ma tean_ -> _Ma ei tea._
  - _Ta tahab_ -> _Ta ei taha._
- **Past Indicative**: _ei_ + _-nud_ participle:
  - _Ma teadsin_ -> _Ma ei teadnud._
  - _Ta ostis_ -> _Ta ei ostnud._
- **Negative Auxiliary _ega_**: Used to reinforce negative coordination or polite negative questions:
  - _Ma ei taha kohvi ega teed._ (I want neither coffee nor tea.)
  - _Ega sa ei ole väsinud?_ (You aren't tired, are you?)
- **Negative Imperative**: Formed with **ära** (sg) / **ärge** (pl):
  - _Ära räägi!_ (Don't speak! - sg)
  - _Ärge kartke!_ (Don't be afraid! - pl/formal)

## 5. Coordination and Subordination (Liitlause)

### Coordinating Conjunctions (Rinnastavad sidendid)

- **ja**: Neutral "and" (_ema ja isa_).
- **ning**: Formal "and", used to connect longer, distinct clauses or to prevent repetition of _ja_ (_Me käisime teatris ning sõime pärast kohvikus_).
- **aga**: "but" (_Tahan tulla, aga mul pole aega_).
- **kuid**: Formal literary "but" (_Ilm oli külm, kuid päikesepaisteline_).
- **vaid**: "but rather / on the contrary" — **Mandatory after a negative clause**:
  - _See pole minu auto, **vaid** naabri oma._ (That isn't my car, but rather the neighbor's.)
  - _Never use `aga` when contradicting a negated premise._

### Subordinating Conjunctions (Alistavad sidendid)

- **et**: "that" / "in order that" (_Ma tean, et sa tuled_; _Õpin, et targaks saada_).
- **sest**: "because" (_Jäin hiljaks, sest buss ei tulnud_).
- **kuna**: "since / as" (_Kuna sadas vihma, jäime koju_).
- **kui**: "when" / "if" (_Helista mulle, kui jõuad koju_).
- **kuigi / ehkki**: "although" (_Läksin välja, kuigi oli külm_).
- **kuni**: "until" (_Ootame, kuni vihm lakkab_).

### Punctuation Rule: Mandatory Comma before Subordinate Clauses

In Estonian, **every subordinate clause must be set off by a comma**, regardless of conjunction length:

- _Ta ütles, et tuleb._ (He said that he will come.)
- _Kui ilm on ilus, läheme randa._ (If the weather is nice, we go to the beach.)
