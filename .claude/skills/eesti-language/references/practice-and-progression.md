# Practice, CEFR Progression, and Learner Pitfalls (Keeleõpe ja veaanalüüs)

Token-minimized reference for curriculum progression, diagnostic benchmarks, and error mitigation (drawing from official Harno reference level descriptions and empirical learner corpora).

---

## 1. CEFR Level Expectations (Harno Benchmarks)

| Level | Morphosyntax Focus | Vocabulary Scope | Communicative Tasks & Exams |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A1** | Present indicative; basic personal pronouns; basic numbers; spatial question words (*kus, kuhu, kust*); basic nominative, genitive, and partitive singular. | ~750 words (basic everyday items) | Self-introduction, asking for prices, ordering in a cafe, telling the time, simple greetings. |
| **A2** | **All 14 cases** in singular and plural; short illatives (*koju, linna, kooli*); simple past (*lihtminevik*); perfect (*täisminevik*); **impersonal present and past** (*räägitakse, tehti*); *ma*- vs. *da*-infinitive; **total vs. partial object**; *et*- and *sest*-clauses. | ~1,500–2,000 words | Describing daily routine, shopping, asking directions, filling out forms, writing short messages (>=30 words), speaking in pairs from prompt cards. |
| **B1** | Full mastery of object case rules; **quotative mood** (*-vat*); conditional in polite requests and reported speech; passive participles (*-tud*); relative clauses; compound past tenses. | ~4,500 words | Telephone inquiries, handling institutional appointments, writing personal and semi-formal letters (~100 words), reaching mutual agreement in discussion. |
| **B2** | Non-finite converb clauses (*des*- and *mata*-forms); full nuance of particle verbs (*ära, läbi, välja, üle*); formal register shifts; complex journalistic and academic styles. | ~7,500+ words | Explaining nuanced viewpoints, writing argumentative essays (~180 words), participating in spontaneous debates, summarizing technical audio. |

## 2. High-Frequency Learner Pitfalls & Interference

### 1. The Slavic Aspect Trap
- **The Error**: Looking for verbal prefixes to express perfective vs. imperfective aspect (e.g. attempting to translate Russian *написать* vs. *писать* through verbal mutation).
- **The Fix**: Estonian encodes aspect primarily via the **direct object**:
  - Unfinished / ongoing: Partitive (*Lugesin raamatut* / *Kirjutasin artiklit*).
  - Finished / complete: Genitive total object (*Lugesin raamatu läbi* / *Kirjutasin kirja valmis*).

### 2. The Negative Object Error
- **The Error**: Leaving the direct object in the Nominative or Genitive in a negative clause:
  - `*Ma ei ostnud uue auto.` (Incorrect)
- **The Fix**: Negation demands the **Partitive** without exception:
  - *Ma ei ostnud uut autot.* (Correct)

### 3. The Imperative Object Error
- **The Error**: Using the Genitive singular for a completed command:
  - `*Võta selle leiva!` (Incorrect)
- **The Fix**: Singular total objects in imperative commands take the **Nominative**:
  - *Võta see leib!* (Correct)
  - *Osta see raamat!* (Correct)

### 4. Over-Declining Adjectives in the Last 4 Cases (-ni, -na, -ta, -ga)
- **The Error**: Adding the case ending to both the adjective and the noun:
  - `*külmata veeta` (Incorrect)
  - `*uuega autoga` (Incorrect)
- **The Fix**: In the Terminative, Essive, Abessive, and Comitative cases, the adjective **must remain in the Genitive**:
  - *külma veeta* (Correct)
  - *uue autoga* (Correct)

### 5. English Dummy Pronouns & Inversion Failure
- **The Error**: Calquing English dummy "it": `*See sajab vihma.`
- **The Fix**: Estonian weather verbs are impersonal and require no subject: *Sajab vihma.*
- **The Error**: Failing V2 inversion after a fronted adverbial: `*Eile mina käisin...`
- **The Fix**: Invert finite verb and subject: *Eile käisin mina...*

### 6. Finnish False Friends (Petlikud sõbrad)
Learners with Finnish background often confuse crucial lexical pairs:

| Estonian Word | Estonian Meaning | Finnish False Friend | Finnish Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *pulmad* (pl.) | wedding | *pulma* (sg.) | problem |
| *hallitus* | mold / mildew | *hallitus* | government |
| *raamat* | book | *raamattu* | Bible |
| *piim* | milk | *piimä* | sour milk / buttermilk |
| *linn* | city / town | *linna* | castle / fortress |
| *viiner* | hot dog sausage | *viineri* | Danish pastry |

## 3. Targeted Diagnostic Practice Drills

### Drill A: Object Case Selection
Fill the blank with the correct form of *leib* (bread: *leib / leiva / leiba*):
1. *Ma ostan poest värsket _____.* (Answer: *leiba* — mass noun / indefinite quantity).
2. *Söö see _____ ära!* (Answer: *leib* — imperative total object).
3. *Ma sõin terve _____ ära.* (Answer: *leiva* — positive completed action, singular total object).
4. *Ma ei taha _____.* (Answer: *leiba* — negative sentence).

### Drill B: Locative Triad Selection
Fill the blank with the correct form of *pood* (shop: *pood / poe / poodi*):
1. *Kuhu sa lähed? - Lähen _____.* (Answer: *poodi* — short illative destination).
2. *Kus sa oled? - Olen _____.* (Answer: *poes* — inessive location).
3. *Kust sa tuled? - Tulen _____.* (Answer: *poest* — elative origin).

### Drill C: *ma*- vs. *da*-Infinitive
Fill the blank with the correct infinitive of *lugema* (to read: *lugema* or *lugeda*):
1. *Ma tahan seda raamatut _____.* (Answer: *lugeda* — modal *tahtma* requires *da*).
2. *Laps läheb voodisse _____.* (Answer: *lugema* — motion verb *minema* requires *ma*).
3. *Ta hakkas eesti keelt _____.* (Answer: *õppima* — *hakkama* requires *ma*).
4. *Kas siin tohib lehte _____?* (Answer: *lugeda* — modal *tohtima* requires *da*).
