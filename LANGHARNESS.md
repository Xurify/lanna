# LangHarness

A fork of LearnHarness 0.3.8 for learning Slovak (and later Russian) from
English and Estonian. Built on branch `slovak` from the plan in
`wayfinder-slovak-harness-spec.md`.

## What it adds

| Area | What you get |
|---|---|
| Language profiles | Slovak and Russian. Each keeps its own sessions, graph and review deck under `data/profiles/<language>/`. Switch with the toggle at the top of the sidebar. The desktop app restarts itself; under `npm run dev`, restart the server. |
| Seeded grammar graph | A fresh Slovak profile starts with 53 grammar nodes (phonetics, cases, verbs, aspect, word order…) and 40 vocabulary clusters, linked by 57 prerequisite chains. Every grammar node carries an Estonian bridge. Seeding runs once; nodes you delete stay deleted. |
| Prompts | `src/agent/prompts/language/10-<language>.md` joins every agent's prompt. It covers the English and Estonian bridges (with where each breaks), adaptive immersion, grading diacritic slips apart from grammar, Russian false friends, and the `lookup_word` rule. Prober, teacher and practice each get a language fragment too. Edit them all in Settings → AI models → Advanced → System prompts. |
| Dictionary-checked forms | `lookup_word` searches the 7,074-word Slovak lexicon exported from slovak.wiki, by form, by unaccented spelling, or by English gloss. The tutor is told to look a word up before it states a form. |
| Audio | Any Slovak in a tutor reply written as `` `say: Dobrý deň` `` gets a play button. Speech tries ElevenLabs, then Microsoft Edge voices, then Piper, then the browser's own voice. Every clip is kept in `data/audio/<lang>/<voice>/<text-slug>-<key>.mp3` and listed in `data/audio/index.jsonl`, so a sentence is paid for once. Speed is 0.75×, 1× or 1.25×; the snail button plays one sentence slower (0.7×, or 0.6× when your speed is 0.75×) and leaves the speed setting alone. Louder voices are turned down to one level, so clips from different voices sound alike. slovak.wiki's clips play as recorded: they are made slowly on purpose, for learners. Volume is a slider in Settings → Sound; in a lesson or Review, hovering or right-clicking the speaker button (or pressing and holding it on a touch screen) drops a vertical slider, and a click still mutes. |
| Storage | Settings → Data lists what the app keeps on this computer: voice clips, the active language's progress and the reset backups, each with its folder and size. On the desktop app, **Clear cache** empties the app's own copy of audio and pages, which downloads again when needed. Saved clips, progress and backups are never cleared from Settings. The web app cannot clear the browser's cache, so it says where to find it. |
| Letter bar | One click types á ä č ď é í ĺ ľ ň ó ô ŕ š ť ú ý ž into whichever field you used last. Shift types the capital. It folds away. |
| Spaced review | The **Review** page in the sidebar is a Clozemaster-style deck: a real Tatoeba sentence with one word blanked, typed or multiple choice, scheduled with FSRS-5. Missing only accents counts as Hard, not wrong. New words come in frequency order, beginner words first, 10 a day by default. The tutor can add words from a lesson to the deck (`add_to_review`) and see how the deck is going (`review_status`). |

## The redesign (October 2026)

Built after a UX audit of a real 70-minute session, which spent 23 minutes
on placement, 37 minutes waiting at the plan approval and 9 minutes teaching.

| Area | What changed |
|---|---|
| Today (`/`) | The home screen. A greeting; four indicators (days in a row with a flag, today's minutes as a ring against your daily goal, words known, XP); a ridge line showing the whole way to your target level, today's plan with one Start on the next step (each task carries the trail mark of its kind of practice), every other way to practice grouped by kind, and the climb: the stage you are on, chapters as stops on the trail, with the current chapter's units under it, and the Stamps card (the last stamp earned, the three closest). The old graph home moved to `/map`. |
| Chapter exam (`/exam?chapter=<id>`) | Eight choose items drawn from the chapter's own words and drills, with the lesson's options and feedback. The pass mark is 80%. Opened from the last row of an open chapter on the path, which also shows the best score so far. Each attempt is kept per chapter in `progress.json`. Record only: nothing is locked by it yet. |
| Grammar atlas (`/grammar`) | The six Slovak cases down the side, the three genders across the top. Each cell is the drill that teaches it and shows how often you were right **on the first try, with no help** — the answer before the cue, not the retry it earned; "not yet" until you have tried it (or no drill exists). Point at a cell for its rule and what went wrong: the wrong first tries named as *dictionary form, no ending* / *wrong ending* / *other word* / *no answer*, the accent-only misses counted apart, and the slips you said in Speak. A place name (Bratislava, Košice) drills the cases but has no gender in the dictionary, so it fills no cell. The tutor reads the same measure as its "Measured weak points". |
| Lessons (`/lesson?unit=`) | A full-screen lesson of 12 reps built from the lexicon on the spot, so no rep waits on a model. Reps mix choose (keys 1–4), type, build the sentence, and listen. Each answer gets green, amber or red feedback with the rule and a Why? button that asks the tutor. **I don't know** (always on the footer, outside Blitz) shows the answer at once: it counts as a miss with no XP, and the rep comes back once at the end. Combos, sounds, confetti and XP. Missed words go into the review deck. |
| Blitz (`/lesson?mode=blitz`) | 60 seconds of choose reps across the units you have started, with a best score. |
| Progress | `data/profiles/<language>/progress.json` holds XP, days, unit levels, the streak and the stamps with the day each was earned. One rest day is forgiven after six active days. Each day also keeps its minutes per activity (`minutesBy`), measured while you were active: the gaps between your actions for lessons, Review, Speak and the like, the time the tab is visible and focused for Read and Listen, and for Watch the time its video plays, plus the time a paused line waits for your decision (up to 2 minutes a line), and the questions (up to 90 seconds after your last action), with the tab visible and focused. |
| Review | A single Continue button takes the suggested rating (Enter). The four ratings stay as a quiet override row. Reviews earn XP. |
| Setup (`/start`) | The minute that replaced the placement chat (October 2026). Four screens: what Slovak is for (can-do purposes that set the target and the Talk scenes), what you bring (the languages you know with a CEFR level each, how much Slovak), minutes a day and days a week (with the date that pace reaches the target), and the placement check, rebuilt on 2026-10-28 after the fixed staircase proved "clearly wrong" (eight items a tier, pass at six, half of them multiple choice, one dictionary word accepted per blank). Now three stages (`src/core/placement-ladder.ts`, `src/server/placement.ts`): an adaptive ladder over the sixteen drill units — every typed item is chosen from the answers before it (a rung held jumps halfway up the open bracket, a gap halves it down, one each asks a third), it enters where you said you stand and closes the bracket in about a dozen items, and a typed answer the dictionary did not expect goes to the cheap model slot, which accepts a valid alternative ("Zostal sam doma"); then the Socratic stage, where the tutor (strong slot) asks up to six production questions on the units at the edge, judging each held / slip / gap, and its verdicts replace the drill's on those units; then a two-minute yes/no word check (forty words across five frequency bands plus eight pseudowords, X_Lex style) that measures vocabulary instead of inferring it from grammar. The result shows the tiers, the tutor's reading of the edge, the words by band, and every miss as evidence. Units you hold are tested out, the chapters behind your edge are skipped (open to revisit), misses come first in the daily plan. A re-check starts from your current chapter and never places you below it. Every item, judgement, turn and tick is kept under `data/profiles/slovak/placement/`, and the check's model calls show in Settings → Spending. The tutor gets all of it as a "This learner" block in its prompt. |
| Tutor chat | Agent machinery (file reads, map lookups, reasoning) is hidden behind a Working toggle. Answers are one tap or one number key; confidence and thinking sit behind "Explain my reasoning". Placement is capped at about 5 questions, a question you write in the thinking field gets answered first, and teaching starts without a plan approval. |
| Talk and Story | Talk in writing (Today's Talk tile) is the Speak screen with the keyboard on (`/speak?mode=typed`): the same six scenes, the tutor answers out loud, you type each line and press Enter, and Koniec gets your corrections. Story is Read, the Robo episode with its questions. Neither has a chat any more (`docs/DECISIONS.md`, E8). The daily plan's speaking step is the spoken Speak task below. |
| Speak (`/speak`) | The output strand, said out loud (October 2026). A scene from what Slovak is for (café, the street, a new colleague, the market, the doctor, family; the chapter's own scene and your purposes' scenes come first). The tutor plays the other person on the cheap model slot: every line arrives as audio and text, the meaning a tap away. You hold the mic, say your line, and it is transcribed (ElevenLabs Scribe with the ElevenLabs key, else Deepgram; typing is always there) and sent as it was heard. With an Azure Speech key every spoken line is also scored sound by sound (accuracy, fluency, completeness and, where the locale has it, prosody — stress and rhythm, the nearest honest measure of accent): a chip on the line, +2 XP for each line said clearly, the scene's average at the end. A slip in a **case ending, verb ending or aspect** comes back as a prompt to repair yourself — *(do škol…? skús ešte raz)* — never as a silent recast (prompts change grammar, recasts are not noticed); everything else waits. After about eight lines, or when the scene ends, or on *Koniec*: at most three corrections (yours → the right line, the rule in a few words, played aloud), two phrases to keep, the slips' words into the review deck. Every scene is kept once under `data/profiles/<language>/speak/` with your own recordings line by line. |
| Speed (`/fluency`) | The fluency strand (October 2026): nothing new, only faster. **Read it faster** times you re-reading an episode you have already read, shows words a minute against your last time on it (or the level's target), then asks one of its questions. **Say it faster** gives six sentences every word of which you know (the ones you got right in lessons first), each heard once and then said three times in a shrinking window; with the microphone on, a pass ends when your voice does and is scored before the verdict: **a pass counts only if it was inside the window AND clear** (70 or more — sound by sound with an Azure Speech key, else the words speech-to-text could make out), so a fast mumble does not pass. Both end screens list the sounds and words that keep scoring low across your recent lines, with a play button on each. The plan alternates the two once an episode has been read; Blitz stays. All local, no model. Rounds go to `data/profiles/<language>/fluency/log.jsonl`. |
| Read (`/read`) | A reading task (October 2026): one episode a day of *Robo in Bratislava*, written at your level on the cheap model slot (6–8 sentences at A1 up to 16–20 at C1, about 97 of 100 words you know, 2–3 new words each used three times, a setting drawn from what Slovak is for). Tap any word for its lemma, meaning and tags; the speaker plays a sentence, Play all reads the episode; a translation per sentence on demand. Three meaning-only questions follow, then the new words and whatever you looked up go into the review deck. Every episode is kept once under `data/profiles/<language>/stories/` with its glossed tokens, questions and cost; reading it again is free. The library lists every episode with your score. |
| Listen (`/listen`) | The input task (October 2026, from the comprehensible-input research): ten real dictionary sentences you can follow, each heard first with the text hidden, then typed as the Slovak you heard (dictation, graded like a lesson's listening rep; a sentence with only a missing accent earns half XP). "Didn't catch it" counts the sentence as missed without typing anything. Picking a meaning from three was removed in November 2026. Then shown with its one new word marked and glossed. One new word a sentence at most; the ten together keep at least 93% of their words known (the Read task's own known-words estimate). Typed answers are graded by the server. For the speed measure a sentence is timed by its clip and the plays it took, not by the typing. No grammar grading. New words go into the review deck; the session goes to the lesson log as mode `listen`. Built locally, no model; audio from the cached library. |
| Watch (`/watch`) | Paste a Slovak YouTube link (October 2026: "double subtitles, so I see both as I watch"). The app fetches the video's Slovak captions (YouTube's own, made by people or automatic; yt-dlp when the app cannot reach them; with yt-dlp and a Deepgram key, a video with no captions can be transcribed from its audio on request), cuts them into lines the ear can hold, translates them once on the cheap model slot, glosses every word against the lexicon with the same known-words estimate the Read and Listen tasks use, and writes a comprehension check at your level. Before watching: how much of the video you know (93% is where listening works on its own) and the unknown words worth meeting first, with their sound. With no Slovak captions, the Czech ones are used and the video is labelled Czech (Czech is close to Slovak, but its words are not Slovak). The video plays in YouTube's own embedded player, nothing downloaded. Line by line (the default): each line plays with the text hidden and the video pauses; replay (R), slow it down (S), show the Slovak (T), show the meaning (M), then Got it (Enter) or Not yet (N). Straight through: both subtitles live, the transcript below, click a line to jump. Every word tappable. The end screen shows the lines got on the first unaided listen and the words a minute of that speech (the listening-speed signal), the check, and the words now in your deck. Then "Use the words" (a Slovak video only): one sentence for each of up to three words, the ones you tapped first and then the video's own new words. A program checks each sentence first, then the cheap model slot judges it; the meaning shows after your first try, and a word earns 5 XP the first time it is accepted for that video. A video you added waits on Today's plan as a Watch task until watched; watching counts toward the input strand. Everything is kept under `data/profiles/<language>/videos/` (the caption track as fetched, the lines, the questions, every watching with per-line stats). |
| Desirable difficulties | The standing rule (2026-10-28: "things should be hard… that is where the learning happens"), written up in `docs/research/desirable-difficulties.md`. Every rep is retrieval first, help is graded and priced, difficulty rises with mastery. Lessons: the first miss shows only the smallest cue (the trigger word, or which form is wanted); the rule and bridges come with the answer after the second miss, opened by themselves. A missed choice retries typed. From the practiced stage on, the English is hidden until the answer ("Meaning · half XP" shows it early). A complete unit is **strong**: production only, six reps from the units it is confused with, meaning hidden. Review: the hint is graded (meaning, then the word) and caps the rating (Good, then Hard); a card already retrieved once is always typed, and a picked answer is at most Good. Listen: "Show the text" waits for a second listen and that sentence does not count as understood by ear. Watch: the meaning needs the Slovak shown first. |
| Input before output | A new unit's first lesson opens with two "What does it mean?" reps: the whole sentence spoken, the target form marked, three meanings to pick from. The choose and type reps follow; once a unit is practiced the lesson is production almost throughout. |
| When can I…? (`/milestones`) | The calculator (October 2026: "when will I understand most of a Slovak YouTube video?"). Six situations — a casual vlog, the news, a café or shop, the in-laws, a film with and without subtitles — plus any you add in your own words (mapped once by the cheap model slot to a kind of language, a coverage target, a speaker rate and the support the situation gives; kept in `data/profiles/<language>/milestones.json`). For each, two gaps measured separately: **words** (your working vocabulary — the deck and word lessons, plus the commonest words your level implies — against the lemmas the kind of language needs for 95% or 98% of its running words, from Nation 2006, Webb & Rodgers 2009, Adolphs & Schmitt 2003, raised a quarter for Slovak's morphology) and **speed** (words a minute of understanding from the Listen task's per-sentence timing and, when it exists, the Watch task, against the speaker's rate discounted for pictures, subtitles or the right to ask again). Dates at your planned minutes and at your real two-week pace; the later gap decides. Speed grows along the guided-hours ladder anchored to the CEFR listening descriptors until eight of your own Listen sessions replace it with your trend. Evidence panel, a timeline, the next words that pay most (one tap into Review), every study cited. Opened from Today's goal line and from the hours sheet. |
| This week's balance | Under the daily plan: the week's minutes by Nation's four strands (input, speaking, words & rules, speed) against a quarter each, with the strand furthest behind named. The minutes are measured while you were active; Talk, Story and tutor sessions have no timer, and days before minutes were measured never had one, so those count each activity's planned minutes, and the card says how many. A tight daily budget keeps one task per strand (Listen, Speak, Read, Speed) before a second drill. The research note behind all three is the "Comprehensible Input and LangHarness" page. |
| Stamps (`/progress#stamps`) | *Turistické známky*, the round wooden stamps hikers collect at every Slovak peak and hut, as the app's record of outcomes (November 2026, E31, `docs/research/gamification.md`): a summit stamp per level, a stamp per chapter, and 42 deeds (your first lesson; 7, 30, 100 and 365 days in a row; 50 to 3,000 words known; 100 to 5,000 review answers; lessons without a miss; words once missed, now known; a week with all four kinds of practice; stories read; scenes said aloud; listening sessions; Blitz scores; hours on the trail; chapter exams passed). A stamp is earned by an outcome on record, never by an action; it carries no XP, buys nothing, and once earned keeps its day. A lesson that earns one presses it onto the end screen. Today's Stamps card shows the last one earned and the three closest with their counts; the book on Progress shows them all in three groups. The This week card counts the days practiced against the days a week in your goal and says when the target is met. Progress also keeps a trail log: hours on the trail, words known, days practiced, longest streak, lessons, review answers, stories read, scenes said aloud. No leaderboards, hearts or currency, by rule. |

Originals of every file the redesign changed are in

| Your data, kept | Everything the app generates or you produce stays on disk in open formats, with a README in every folder: `data/README.md` is the map. ## Phone and iPad (November 2026)

The screens lay out for a phone (390 px wide) and an iPad (820 px in portrait), with touch targets at least 44 px tall. A phone or iPad reaches the app over your own Tailscale network, once you turn on **Remote access** in Settings → Devices and pair the device with a code. The server still listens on this computer only: Tailscale Serve connects from here. Remote access is off by default. The owner's steps are in [docs/remote-access.md](docs/remote-access.md), and the route is in [research/mobile-ipad.md](docs/research/mobile-ipad.md).

| Area | What you get |
|---|---|
| Layout | Today, lessons, review, Read and Speak fit a phone and an iPad with no sideways scroll. On a phone, or a touch screen narrower than 1024 px, the sidebar is a drawer over the page, closed until you open it. |
| Touch | Sidebar rows and buttons, the reader's header and bottom bar, and the leave buttons are at least 44 px. Keycap hints (`Enter`, `1`–`4`) show only where a fine pointer is the main input. |
| Read | The tap-to-gloss card stays inside the screen edges on a narrow screen. |
| Speak | On touch, press and hold the mic button to talk and let go to send. The keyboard and mouse toggle is unchanged. |
| Home screen | The app has a web-app manifest and icons, so Share, then Add to Home Screen, opens it full screen. From a phone it works once the device is paired (see Devices). |
| Notch and home bar | The top bar and the drawer clear the notch and the home indicator. |
| Devices (Settings → Devices) | Off by default. Turn on **Remote access**, run `tailscale serve --bg 3010` once on this computer (Tailscale signed in, HTTPS certificates on), then choose **Make a code** and type the six digits on the phone within five minutes. Each paired device is listed with its last use and can be signed out on its own; turning remote access off signs out every device. This computer's own browser never asks for a code. Funnel stays off. Steps: [docs/remote-access.md](docs/remote-access.md). |

## Run it

```
npm install
npm run dev          # http://localhost:3010
```

Add a model key in Settings → API keys as in LearnHarness.

Optional speech keys (Settings → API keys → Speech, or `.env.local`):

- `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`: the best voice, and speech-to-text for the Speak task (Scribe). Change the voice with `ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID_SK` or `ELEVENLABS_VOICE_ID`.
- `DEEPGRAM_API_KEY`: speech-to-text for the Speak task when there is no ElevenLabs key (and the composer's dictation).
- `AZURE_SPEECH_KEY` + `AZURE_SPEECH_REGION`: Azure Speech pronunciation assessment (Slovak is covered) for Speak and Say it faster — a phoneme-level score for every spoken line. Without them clarity is judged from the transcript alone.
- `LH_PRON_SCRIPT=1`: dev only — a made-up pronunciation score for working on the screens without a key. `LH_STT_SCRIPT=<text>`: dev only — every recording transcribes to that text.
- `LH_SPEAK_SCRIPT=<file>`: dev only — a JSON array of scripted tutor replies for working on the Speak screen without a model.
- Edge voices need no key, only internet.
- Piper works offline: set `PIPER_PATH` to the binary and `PIPER_MODEL_SK` to a Slovak `.onnx` voice.

On Windows, `npm test` and `npm run desktop:dev` use Unix-style env syntax
inherited from upstream. Run them from Git Bash, or once run
`npm config set script-shell "C:\Program Files\Git\bin\bash.exe"`.

## Refresh the curriculum

```
npm run lexicon:export   # reads ../slovak.wiki, writes curriculum/slovak/lexicon.json
npm run seed:build       # curriculum/slovak/seed-source.json → seed.json
```

The seed source is hand-written. Edit nodes, chains, clusters or goals
there, then rebuild. An existing profile is not re-seeded. To start
fresh, delete `data/profiles/slovak/`.

## Limits worth knowing

- Only 4 words (from slovak.wiki's hand-checked set) have full declension tables checked against JÚĽŠ. 2,958 more have rule-generated form sets that slovak.wiki verified. For the rest, `lookup_word` says plainly that the form is not in the dictionary.
- Russian has prompts and a profile, but no lexicon, seed or review deck yet.
- Edge speech could not be tested in the build sandbox (no network there). The fallback to the next tier was tested.
- Example sentences come from Tatoeba (CC BY 2.0 FR). The review page credits them.

## Tests

`tests/lesson.test.ts` covers the lesson builder, XP and streaks; `tests/stamps.test.ts`, `tests/stamps-record.test.ts` and `tests/render-stamps.test.tsx` the stamp catalog, the earning rule, a lesson minting its stamps on a throwaway data folder, and the stamp components. `tests/milestones.test.ts` covers the coverage curve, the speed ladder and reading, the estimates and the custom-milestone round trip. `tests/speak.test.ts` covers the spoken scene (the tutor's JSON, prompts and repairs, the summary into the deck, the files) and `tests/fluency.test.ts` the speed rounds (known-sentence picking, the windows, words a minute, the logs). `tests/onboarding.test.ts` covers the purposes, the tier outcome (re-checks hold the tiers below), how placement moves the map, the tutor's profile block and the goal round trip; `tests/placement.test.ts` the adaptive ladder, the model's judgement of typed misses, the Socratic stage's JSON and verdicts, the word check's scoring and pseudowords, and the whole check through the server. `tests/corpus.test.ts` covers the audio library, the lesson log, Why? reuse, chat trash and the export. `tests/langharness.test.ts` covers answer comparison, FSRS-5, the lexicon,
cloze cards, seed integrity (no cycles), prompt composition and the review
service. The full suite passes except one Pi loader test that also fails
on untouched upstream under Node 22.
