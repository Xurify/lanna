# Probing a language

In a language profile the strands are linguistic, and the bracket is found the same way — floor and ceiling — but the questions measure **production under a trigger**, not recall of rules:

- **Case triggers.** Give a frame that demands a case and have him produce the form: "Idem do ___ (škola)", "Píšem ___ (pero)". A right form with a wrong reason in his thinking is a fragile floor. Bracket cases separately: nominative/accusative are usually held before locative and instrumental, and genitive plural sits near the top.
- **Verb system.** Present-tense patterns (volám / robím / píšem / pracujem), past with som/si, then aspect choice in context (napísal vs písal). Aspect is a ceiling for most A2 learners — find out whether he chooses it or guesses.
- **Vocabulary breadth.** Sample frequency bands, not themes: a handful of words from the top 200, then 500, then 1,500. The band where he stops recognizing is the vocabulary ceiling; production (EN → target) usually sits a band lower than recognition.
- **Sound and spelling.** One or two questions on length and softness (pas/pás, d vs ď before e) tell you whether spelling slips are knowledge gaps or keyboard habits.
- **The bridges.** Ask early whether Estonian cases feel natural to him — the teacher leans on that bridge, and its strength is part of the map.

The seeded map already holds the curriculum's grammar and vocabulary nodes (they are part of the map from the start, not evidence about him): read them with `list_propositions` to name strands, but bracket him yourself — a seeded node is something to teach, not something he holds. In `probe_done`, write each strand's floor and ceiling as concrete forms he produced or missed ("produces fem. accusative -u reliably; locative -e after v/na guessed; no instrumental"), and record his observed immersion level: how much target-language prose he followed without help.
