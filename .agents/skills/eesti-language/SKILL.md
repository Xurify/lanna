# Estonian Language (Eesti keel)

Produce idiomatic standard Estonian, explain choices at the learner's level, and preserve the speaker's intended register. Use CEFR levels as flexible guidance, not as a substitute for context-sensitive editing.

## Select the task mode

| Request | Do |
| --- | --- |
| Translate | Establish audience, `sina`/`teie`, and register. Translate meaning and natural collocation, not words one by one. Handle gender-neutral pronouns (`tema`/`ta`) accurately in context. |
| Write or rewrite | State the register, use natural Estonian discourse markers, and proofread case government, grade alternation, object case (total vs. partial), and verb rections. |
| Correct | Return a corrected version first. Then give high-value explanations, labelled by error type (case government, gradation, aspect/object, verb form). Preserve natural colloquial phrasing unless formal standard is requested. |
| Teach | Diagnose the learner's level with a short production task. Teach one controlled pattern (e.g. spatial case triad or _ma_/_da_ contrast), contrast with near misses, then elicit new sentences. |
| Plan study | Map the learner's goal to an A1–B2/C1 progression aligned with official Harno criteria. Combine retrieval, spaced review, listening, speaking, reading, and writing. |
| Explain grammar | Give the functional meaning first, then the minimal paradigm (the 3 principal nominal forms or 4 verb principal parts) and 3–5 contextual examples. |
| Author curriculum | Validate sentences against authentic usage, ensure valid cloze tokens, correct case government, and check all standard variant answers. |

Never omit Estonian diacritics in final text (`ä, ö, ü, õ, š, ž`). Use `eesti keel` for the language and `Eesti` for Estonia.

## Mandatory production pass

Before finalizing meaningful Estonian output, check:

1. **Stem selection & gradation (astmevaheldus):** Verify whether each noun or verb form requires the strong (_tugev_) or weak (_nõrk_) grade (`tuba : toa`, `mõte : mõtte`, `lugema : loen`, `hakkama : hakkan`). Never build forms by analogy without checking gradation type.
2. **Object marking (täissihitis vs. osasihitis):** Check whether the object must be in the partitive (_osasihitis_) or total object case (_täissihitis_ — nominative or genitive). Remember: negative sentences ALWAYS require partitive objects (`Ma ei ostnud raamatut`).
3. **Government (rektsioon):** Ensure verbs and adpositions govern their mandatory case (`aitama + partitive`, `meeldima + allative`, `hakkama + ma-infinitive`, `nõustuma + ma-infinitive`, `ilma + abessive/partitive`).
4. **Locative triads (sise- and väliskohakäänded):** Distinguish origin, state, and destination clearly: internal `kuhu? kus? kust?` (_majja, majas, majast_) vs. external (_lauale, laual, laualt_). Use correct municipal locative traditions (_Tallinnas, Tartus, Pärnus_ vs. _Tapal, Elvas, Otepääl_).
5. **Verb forms & infinitives:** Check _ma_-infinitive (goal/action start) vs. _da_-infinitive (modal/ability/desire). Impersonal forms (_umbisikuline_) require correct _-takse/-ti/-tud_ or _-dakse/-di/-dud_ stems.
6. **Word order:** Main declarative clauses generally follow verb-second (V2) order. Inverted order in existential sentences introduces new information at the end (_Laual on leib_ vs. _Leib on laual_).
7. **Interference:** Avoid Russian interference (do not look for verbal aspect prefixes; use Estonian particle verbs and object case instead), English interference (no calqued dummy pronouns or articles), and Finnish false friends (_hallitus_ = mold in Estonian vs. government in Finnish; _linn_ = city/town in Estonian vs. Finnish _linna_ castle).
8. **Spelling & length:** Distinguish short, long, and overlong sounds (_kodu / kood / koodi_; _lina / linna / linna_); observe consonant letters `b, d, g` (short unvoiced) vs. `p, t, k` (long unvoiced) vs. `pp, tt, kk` (overlong).

## Verify instead of guessing

When any form, gradation class, government, or idiom is uncertain:

1. **Sõnaveeb (EKI)** at [sonaveeb.ee](https://sonaveeb.ee/): The official unified dictionary of the Institute of the Estonian Language (_Eesti Keele Instituut_). Checks inflection types, paradigms, government (_rektsioon_), collocations, and register.
2. **Eesti keele käsiraamat (EKKR)** at [arhiiv.eki.ee/books/ekkr/](https://arhiiv.eki.ee/books/ekkr/): Definitive normative reference for morphology, syntax, word formation, and orthography (M. Erelt, T. Erelt, K. Ross).
3. **Eesti keele süntaks (Erelt & Metslang 2017)**: Exhaustive reference for complex clause structures, secondary predicates, evidentials, and converbs.
4. **Eesti keele põhisõnavara sõnastik** at [eki.ee/dict/psv/](https://www.eki.ee/dict/psv/): Level-graded learner dictionary (A1–B2) with frequency-based examples.

If you cannot verify, indicate uncertainty explicitly and offer the safest standard alternative. Never invent an inflectional grade alternation or suffix.

## Teaching defaults

- **Three base forms first:** Always teach nouns with their three foundational forms: _nimetav, omastav, osastav_ (e.g. `tuba, toa, tuba` / `raamat, raamatu, raamatut`). All other 11 cases build systematically upon either the genitive or partitive stem.
- **Four verb parts first:** Always introduce verbs with their four principal forms: _ma_-infinitive, _da_-infinitive, 1sg present, and 3sg past (e.g. `lugema, lugeda, loen, luges` / `nägema, näha, näen, nägi`).
- **Complete communicative frames:** Teach cases in natural phrases rather than bare case tables: `Ma elan Tallinnas`, `Ma sõidan maale`, `Klaas on piima täis`.
- **Contrastive pairs:** Contrast the spatial opposites immediately: `bussile / bussilt / bussil`, `poodi / poest / poes`.
- **Elicit production:** Require the learner to produce a tailored sentence with the targeted case or verb form before introducing additional nuances.
