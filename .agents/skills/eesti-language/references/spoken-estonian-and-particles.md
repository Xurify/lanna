# Spoken Estonian, Discourse Particles, and Register (Suuline kõne ja partiklid)

Token-minimized reference for dialogue markers, spoken contractions, and conversational register.

---

## 1. High-Frequency Discourse Particles

Estonian dialogue relies heavily on pragmatic particles to express speaker attitude, evidentiality, and expectation:

| Particle | Meaning / Function | Contextual Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ju** | "You know", "after all", appeals to shared knowledge | *Sa tead seda ju küll.* (You know that after all.)<br>*Ma ütlesin ju sulle.* (I told you so, didn't I?) |
| **küll** | 1. Reassurance ("surely, definitely")<br>2. Concession ("indeed... but") | 1. *Küll me hakkama saame.* (We'll manage just fine.)<br>2. *Ta tahtis küll tulla, aga ei jõudnud.* (He did want to come, but couldn't make it.) |
| **ometi** | "Finally", "at last", or insistence/impatience | *Tule ometi siia!* (Do come here at last!)<br>*Ometi kord on reede.* (Finally it's Friday.) |
| **vist** | "Probably", "I suppose" | *Ta on vist kodus.* (He is probably at home.) |
| **ehk** | "Perhaps", "maybe" (polite softeners) | *Ehk saame kokku leppida?* (Perhaps we can agree?) |
| **vaata / kuule** | Attention-getters ("Look", "Listen") | *Kuule, kas sul on hetk aega?* (Listen, do you have a second?) |
| **noh** | Conversational pause, turn-taking filler | *Noh, hakkame siis minema.* (Well, let's get going then.) |
| **nagu** | "Like", approximate hedging / word-search marker | *See oli nagu natuke imelik.* (It was, like, a bit strange.) |

## 2. Conversational Tags & Affirmations

### The Question Tag (*või / vä / vää*)
In colloquial speech, yes/no questions are often formed by dropping *kas* and appending *või* or the casual clitic *vä*:
- *Tuled kaasa või?* (Coming along, or what?)
- *Nalja teed vä?* (Are you kidding?)
- *Said aru vä?* (Did you understand?)

### Affirmative & Negative Responses
- **jah**: Standard factual "yes".
- **jaa**: Warmer, attentive, emphatic agreement ("yes, indeed; gladly").
- **mhmh**: Extremely common low-register vocalic assent in casual dialogue.
- **ei**: "no". Fast double negation *eiei* softens refusals ("no no, it's fine").
- **muidugi / kindlasti**: "of course / certainly".

## 3. Spoken Reductions & Contractions (Kõnepruugi lühendid)

In authentic spontaneous speech, several morphological clusters contract regularly:

| Standard Written Form | Spoken / Casual Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *ei ole* | *pole* (standard spoken) / *ple* (rapid text) | is not / are not |
| *ei tea* | *ma'i tea* / *eitea* | I don't know |
| *olnud* | *olnd* | been (past participle) |
| *läinud* | *läind* | gone |
| *tulnud* | *tulnd* | come |
| *võinud* | *võind* | could have |
| *hakkama* | *akkama* (initial *h-* dropped in casual speech) | to begin |
| *hommikul* | *ommikul* | in the morning |

## 4. Register & Forms of Address (Sina vs. Teie)

### Sinatamine (*sina / sa*)
- Universal among family, friends, children, students, and peers.
- Standard culture in modern Estonian startups, creative industries, and casual commerce.
- Uses 2sg verb forms: *Mis sa teed?*, *Kuidas sul läheb?*

### Teietamine (*teie / te*)
- Respectful form when addressing unfamiliar adults, elderly persons, medical professionals, or official authorities.
- Uses 2pl verb forms: *Kuidas teil läheb?*, *Kas te saaksite aidata?*
- Capitalized *Teie* is reserved for formal personal correspondence (letters and formal emails).

### Everyday Politeness Formulas
- **Aitäh! / Tänan!**: "Thank you!"
- **Suur aitäh! / Tänan väga!**: "Thank you very much!"
- **Palun!**: "Please!" / "Here you are!" / "You're welcome!"
- **Pole tänu väärt! / Võta heaks!**: "Don't mention it!" / "You're welcome!"
- **Vabanda! / Vabandage!**: "Excuse me! / I'm sorry!"
- **Head aega! / Nägemist!**: "Goodbye!"
- **Nägemiseni!**: "See you soon!"
- **Tsau! / Tšau!**: "Hi! / Bye!" (ubiquitous informal greeting).
