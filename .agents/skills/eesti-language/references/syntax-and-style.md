# Syntax, Word Order, and Clause Structure (Süntaks ja lauseõpetus)

Token-minimized reference for clause architecture based on Erelt & Metslang (*Eesti keele süntaks*, 2017).

---

## 1. Constituent Order & the V2 Principle

In neutral declarative main clauses, standard Estonian adheres to the **Verb-Second (V2)** constraint: the finite verb occupies the second structural position in the clause.

### Constituent Permutations
- **SVO (Neutral topic: Subject)**:
  - *Jüri* [1] *ostis* [V] *eile poest uue raamatu.* (Jüri bought a new book from the shop yesterday.)
- **Adv-V-S-O (Inverted topic: Adverbial)**:
  - *Eile* [1] *ostis* [V] *Jüri poest uue raamatu.* (Yesterday, Jüri bought a new book from the shop.)
  - *Poest* [1] *ostis* [V] *Jüri eile uue raamatu.* (From the shop, Jüri bought a new book yesterday.)
- **O-V-S-Adv (Topicalized Object)**:
  - *Uue raamatu* [1] *ostis* [V] *Jüri eile poest.* (The new book, Jüri bought yesterday from the shop.)

*Note: Placing an adverbial at the front automatically moves the subject after the finite verb. Never produce `*Eile Jüri ostis...` in formal standard Estonian.*

## 2. Existential and Possession Clauses

Estonian sharply distinguishes between **normal predicative clauses** and **existential clauses**:

### Normal Predicative vs. Existential Clauses
| Clause Type | Constituent Order | Focus / Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Normal Predicative** | Subject (known) + Verb + Location | Location of a definite entity | *Raamat on laual.* (The book is on the table.) |
| **Existential** | Location + Verb (*on*) + Subject (indefinite) | Existence or introduction of a new entity | *Laual on raamat.* (There is a book on the table.) |

### The Critical Case Shift in Negative Existential & Possession Clauses
In positive existential and possessive sentences, an indefinite subject can be in the Nominative or Partitive. In the **negative**, the subject **always shifts to the Partitive case**:

- **Positive Possession**: *Mul on auto.* (I have a car — Nominative *auto*).
- **Negative Possession**: *Mul ei ole autot.* (I don't have a car — Partitive *autot*).
- **Positive Existential**: *Kapis on leib.* (There is bread in the cupboard — Nominative *leib*).
- **Negative Existential**: *Kapis ei ole leiba.* (There is no bread in the cupboard — Partitive *leiba*).

## 3. Question Formation

Estonian employs three main interrogation strategies:

1. **General (Yes/No) Questions**: Formed by placing the question particle **kas** at the beginning of the declarative clause without changing word order:
   - *Kas sa tuled täna õhtul kinno?* (Are you coming to the cinema tonight?)
   - *Kas te räägite inglise keelt?* (Do you speak English?)
2. **Inverted Questions**: Common in rapid or colloquial speech:
   - *Tuled sa täna?* (Are you coming today?)
3. **Wh-Questions (K-küsimused)**: Fronted interrogative pronoun or adverb + V2:
   - *Kus sa elad?* (Where do you live?)
   - *Mida sa sööd?* (What are you eating?)
   - *Millal rong saabub?* (When does the train arrive?)
   - *Miks sa hilinesid?* (Why were you late?)

## 4. Negation Mechanics

Sentence negation is expressed with the invariant negative particle **ei** accompanied by the **connegative stem**:

- **Present Indicative**: *ei* + connegative verb stem:
  - *Ma tean* -> *Ma ei tea.*
  - *Ta tahab* -> *Ta ei taha.*
- **Past Indicative**: *ei* + *-nud* participle:
  - *Ma teadsin* -> *Ma ei teadnud.*
  - *Ta ostis* -> *Ta ei ostnud.*
- **Negative Auxiliary *ega***: Used to reinforce negative coordination or polite negative questions:
  - *Ma ei taha kohvi ega teed.* (I want neither coffee nor tea.)
  - *Ega sa ei ole väsinud?* (You aren't tired, are you?)
- **Negative Imperative**: Formed with **ära** (sg) / **ärge** (pl):
  - *Ära räägi!* (Don't speak! - sg)
  - *Ärge kartke!* (Don't be afraid! - pl/formal)

## 5. Coordination and Subordination (Liitlause)

### Coordinating Conjunctions (Rinnastavad sidendid)
- **ja**: Neutral "and" (*ema ja isa*).
- **ning**: Formal "and", used to connect longer, distinct clauses or to prevent repetition of *ja* (*Me käisime teatris ning sõime pärast kohvikus*).
- **aga**: "but" (*Tahan tulla, aga mul pole aega*).
- **kuid**: Formal literary "but" (*Ilm oli külm, kuid päikesepaisteline*).
- **vaid**: "but rather / on the contrary" — **Mandatory after a negative clause**:
  - *See pole minu auto, **vaid** naabri oma.* (That isn't my car, but rather the neighbor's.)
  - *Never use `aga` when contradicting a negated premise.*

### Subordinating Conjunctions (Alistavad sidendid)
- **et**: "that" / "in order that" (*Ma tean, et sa tuled*; *Õpin, et targaks saada*).
- **sest**: "because" (*Jäin hiljaks, sest buss ei tulnud*).
- **kuna**: "since / as" (*Kuna sadas vihma, jäime koju*).
- **kui**: "when" / "if" (*Helista mulle, kui jõuad koju*).
- **kuigi / ehkki**: "although" (*Läksin välja, kuigi oli külm*).
- **kuni**: "until" (*Ootame, kuni vihm lakkab*).

### Punctuation Rule: Mandatory Comma before Subordinate Clauses
In Estonian, **every subordinate clause must be set off by a comma**, regardless of conjunction length:
- *Ta ütles, et tuleb.* (He said that he will come.)
- *Kui ilm on ilus, läheme randa.* (If the weather is nice, we go to the beach.)
