# Practice, CEFR Progression, and Learner Pitfalls (Keeleõpe ja veaanalüüs)

Token-minimized reference for curriculum progression, diagnostic benchmarks, and error mitigation (drawing from official Harno reference level descriptions and empirical learner corpora).

---

## 1. CEFR Level Expectations (Harno Benchmarks)

| Level | Morphosyntax Focus | Vocabulary Scope | Communicative Tasks & Exams |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A1** | Present indicative; basic personal pronouns; basic numbers; spatial question words (_kus, kuhu, kust_); basic nominative, genitive, and partitive singular. | ~750 words (basic everyday items) | Self-introduction, asking for prices, ordering in a cafe, telling the time, simple greetings. |
| **A2** | **All 14 cases** in singular and plural; short illatives (_koju, linna, kooli_); simple past (_lihtminevik_); perfect (_täisminevik_); **impersonal present and past** (_räägitakse, tehti_); _ma_- vs. _da_-infinitive; **total vs. partial object**; _et_- and _sest_-clauses. | ~1,500–2,000 words | Describing daily routine, shopping, asking directions, filling out forms, writing short messages (>=30 words), speaking in pairs from prompt cards. |
| **B1** | Full mastery of object case rules; **quotative mood** (_-vat_); conditional in polite requests and reported speech; passive participles (_-tud_); relative clauses; compound past tenses. | ~4,500 words | Telephone inquiries, handling institutional appointments, writing personal and semi-formal letters (~100 words), reaching mutual agreement in discussion. |
| **B2** | Non-finite converb clauses (_des_- and _mata_-forms); full nuance of particle verbs (_ära, läbi, välja, üle_); formal register shifts; complex journalistic and academic styles. | ~7,500+ words | Explaining nuanced viewpoints, writing argumentative essays (~180 words), participating in spontaneous debates, summarizing technical audio. |

## 2. High-Frequency Learner Pitfalls & Interference

### 1. The Slavic Aspect Trap

- **The Error**: Looking for verbal prefixes to express perfective vs. imperfective aspect (e.g. attempting to translate Russian _написать_ vs. _писать_ through verbal mutation).
- **The Fix**: Estonian encodes aspect primarily via the **direct object**:
  - Unfinished / ongoing: Partitive (_Lugesin raamatut_ / _Kirjutasin artiklit_).
  - Finished / complete: Genitive total object (_Lugesin raamatu läbi_ / _Kirjutasin kirja valmis_).

### 2. The Negative Object Error

- **The Error**: Leaving the direct object in the Nominative or Genitive in a negative clause:
  - `*Ma ei ostnud uue auto.` (Incorrect)
- **The Fix**: Negation demands the **Partitive** without exception:
  - _Ma ei ostnud uut autot._ (Correct)

### 3. The Imperative Object Error

- **The Error**: Using the Genitive singular for a completed command:
  - `*Võta selle leiva!` (Incorrect)
- **The Fix**: Singular total objects in imperative commands take the **Nominative**:
  - _Võta see leib!_ (Correct)
  - _Osta see raamat!_ (Correct)

### 4. Over-Declining Adjectives in the Last 4 Cases (-ni, -na, -ta, -ga)

- **The Error**: Adding the case ending to both the adjective and the noun:
  - `*külmata veeta` (Incorrect)
  - `*uuega autoga` (Incorrect)
- **The Fix**: In the Terminative, Essive, Abessive, and Comitative cases, the adjective **must remain in the Genitive**:
  - _külma veeta_ (Correct)
  - _uue autoga_ (Correct)

### 5. English Dummy Pronouns & Inversion Failure

- **The Error**: Calquing English dummy "it": `*See sajab vihma.`
- **The Fix**: Estonian weather verbs are impersonal and require no subject: _Sajab vihma._
- **The Error**: Failing V2 inversion after a fronted adverbial: `*Eile mina käisin...`
- **The Fix**: Invert finite verb and subject: _Eile käisin mina..._

### 6. Finnish False Friends (Petlikud sõbrad)

Learners with Finnish background often confuse crucial lexical pairs:

| Estonian Word | Estonian Meaning | Finnish False Friend | Finnish Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| _pulmad_ (pl.) | wedding | _pulma_ (sg.) | problem |
| _hallitus_ | mold / mildew | _hallitus_ | government |
| _raamat_ | book | _raamattu_ | Bible |
| _piim_ | milk | _piimä_ | sour milk / buttermilk |
| _linn_ | city / town | _linna_ | castle / fortress |
| _viiner_ | hot dog sausage | _viineri_ | Danish pastry |

## 3. Targeted Diagnostic Practice Drills

### Drill A: Object Case Selection

Fill the blank with the correct form of _leib_ (bread: _leib / leiva / leiba_):

1. _Ma ostan poest värsket \_\_\_\_\_._ (Answer: _leiba_ — mass noun / indefinite quantity).
2. _Söö see _____ ära!_ (Answer: _leib_ — imperative total object).
3. _Ma sõin terve _____ ära._ (Answer: _leiva_ — positive completed action, singular total object).
4. _Ma ei taha \_\_\_\_\_._ (Answer: _leiba_ — negative sentence).

### Drill B: Locative Triad Selection

Fill the blank with the correct form of _pood_ (shop: _pood / poe / poodi_):

1. _Kuhu sa lähed? - Lähen \_\_\_\_\_._ (Answer: _poodi_ — short illative destination).
2. _Kus sa oled? - Olen \_\_\_\_\_._ (Answer: _poes_ — inessive location).
3. _Kust sa tuled? - Tulen \_\_\_\_\_._ (Answer: _poest_ — elative origin).

### Drill C: _ma_- vs. _da_-Infinitive

Fill the blank with the correct infinitive of _lugema_ (to read: _lugema_ or _lugeda_):

1. _Ma tahan seda raamatut \_\_\_\_\_._ (Answer: _lugeda_ — modal _tahtma_ requires _da_).
2. _Laps läheb voodisse \_\_\_\_\_._ (Answer: _lugema_ — motion verb _minema_ requires _ma_).
3. _Ta hakkas eesti keelt \_\_\_\_\_._ (Answer: _õppima_ — _hakkama_ requires _ma_).
4. _Kas siin tohib lehte \_\_\_\_\_?_ (Answer: _lugeda_ — modal _tohtima_ requires _da_).
