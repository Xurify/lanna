# Russian Syntax and Style

Russian sentence structure features grammatically flexible word order steered by Functional Sentence Perspective (Topic-Comment structure), impersonal predicate architectures, and nuanced subordinate conjunctions.

## 1. Word Order and Information Structure (FSP)

Because grammatical roles are marked by case endings, Russian word order is functionally pragmatic rather than fixed:

- **Theme / Topic (_Данное_):** Known information placed at the start of the clause.
- **Rheme / Comment (_Новое_):** New, informative, or emphasized information placed at the end of the clause. Intonational center falls on the sentence-final element.

### Definite vs. Indefinite Nuance

Without definite or indefinite articles, Russian uses word order to express definiteness:

- _Студент вошёл в комнату._ (The student entered the room. Topic = the student).
- _В комнату вошёл студент._ (A student entered the room. Comment = a student; introducing a new character).

### Existential vs. Locative Sentences

- **Location of known subject:** `Subject + Verb + Place`:
  - _Книга лежит на столе._ (The book is on the table.)
- **Presence of entity in space (Existential):** `Place + Verb + Subject`:
  - _На столе лежит книга._ (There is a book on the table.)

## 2. Impersonal and Dative Predicates

Russian frequently expresses personal experiences, sensations, moral obligations, and permissions without a nominative subject. The experiencing person is in the **Dative case**:

```
Dative Subject + Predicate (Adverb / Modal) + (Infinitive / Clause)
```

| Type                       | Structure                  | Russian Example                     | English Equivalent                  |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Physical sensation**     | `Dat + Adverb`             | _Мне холодно / жарко / душно._      | I am cold / hot / stifled.          |
| **Psychological state**    | `Dat + Adverb`             | _Ей скучно / весело / грустно._     | She is bored / cheerful / sad.      |
| **Necessity / Obligation** | `Dat + надо / нужно + Inf` | _Нам надо уходить._                 | We must leave.                      |
| **Permission**             | `Dat + можно + Inf`        | _Вам можно войти._                  | You may come in.                    |
| **Prohibition**            | `Dat + нельзя + Inf`       | _Детям нельзя смотреть этот фильм._ | Children must not watch this movie. |
| **Age**                    | `Dat + Number + года/лет`  | _Моему брату двадцать пять лет._    | My brother is 25 years old.         |

_Tense in Impersonal Predicates:_

- Present: zero copula (_Мне холодно. Ему нужно идти._)
- Past: neuter _было_ (_Мне было холодно. Ему нужно было идти._)
- Future: neuter _будет_ (_Мне будет холодно. Ему нужно будет идти._)

## 3. Negation Syntax

### 1. General Sentence Negation with _Не_

Place _не_ immediately before the word being negated:

- _Я **не** знаю этого человека._ (I don't know this person.)
- _Он приехал **не** вчера, а позавчера._ (He arrived not yesterday, but the day before.)

### 2. Negative Pronouns and Double Negation

Russian requires negative pronouns (_никто, ничто, никогда, нигде, никуда, никакой_) to co-occur with _не_ before the verb:

- _Я **ничего не** понимаю._ (I don't understand anything.)
- _Он **никогда никому ничего не** рассказывал._ (He never told anyone anything.)

When combined with prepositions, the negative prefix splits:

- _ни с кем_ (with no one), _ни о чём_ (about nothing), _ни у кого_ (at no one's).

### 3. Negation of Existence (_Нет / Не было / Не будет_)

Governs the **Genitive case**:

- Present: _У меня **нет** словаря._ (I don't have a dictionary.)
- Past: _Вчера в классе **не было** учителя._ (Yesterday the teacher wasn't in class.)
- Future: _Завтра дождя **не будет**._ (Tomorrow there will be no rain.)

## 4. Complex Sentences and Subordination

### 1. Purpose with _Чтобы_

- If the subject of both clauses is the same: `чтобы + Infinitive`:
  - _Я пришёл, **чтобы помочь** вам._ (I came to help you.)
- If the subjects differ: `чтобы + Past Tense`:
  - _Я хочу, **чтобы ты прочитал** эту статью._ (I want you to read this article.)

### 2. Conditionals with _Если_ and _Если бы_

- Real condition: _Если погода будет хорошей, мы пойдём гулять._
- Counterfactual / Unreal condition: _Если бы у меня было время, я поехал бы в Казань._

### 3. Indirect Questions with _Ли_

Particle _ли_ functions as "whether / if", placed immediately after the queried predicate:

- _Я не знаю, **придёт ли** он завтра._ (I don't know whether he will come tomorrow.)
- _Спроси у него, **был ли** он в Москве._ (Ask him whether he has been to Moscow.)
